Wolf Cut vs Butterfly Cut: What’s the Difference?

Wolf Cut

  • Inspired by shag and mullet styles – more rebellious and edgy.
  • Heavy layering and texture, especially at the crown and throughout the length.
  • Often looks best on hair with some natural wave or texture.
  • Great if you want big volume and a “messy cool” aesthetic.

Butterfly Cut

  • Soft, layered cut with face-framing “wings.”
  • Keeps more fullness through the ends and length overall.
  • Works well on straight, wavy, and curly hair when tailored properly.
  • Perfect if you want movement and shape while still feeling feminine and polished.

Which Haircut Suits Me Better?

To answer which haircut suits me better, it helps to think about both your personality and your hair itself – then confirm your guess with side‑by‑side AI previews.

🎯 Personality & Style

If your aesthetic leans more edgy, alternative, or street‑style, you might gravitate toward the wild, tousled look of a wolf cut. If you prefer soft glam, 90s blowouts, or romantic layers, the butterfly cut may feel more aligned with your vibe.

🎯 Hair Type & Daily Routine

The wolf cut can be surprisingly low‑maintenance if you have natural texture and like a “perfectly imperfect” finish. The butterfly cut shines when you enjoy blow‑drying or curling your hair to show off the layered wings. If you rarely style beyond air‑drying, a softer wolf cut or lightly layered butterfly cut may be better than the most dramatic versions you see online.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between wolf cut and butterfly cut?

The wolf cut focuses on choppy, textured layers that create volume and a slightly wild shape, whereas the butterfly cut emphasizes smooth, layered wings around the face while preserving more length and softness overall.

Is wolf cut or butterfly cut easier to maintain?

A softer wolf cut can be easier if you like natural texture and minimal styling, while a full butterfly cut often looks best with blow‑drying or curling. However, you can ask your stylist to customize either cut for lower maintenance.

Do I have to choose just one, or can I combine elements of both?
